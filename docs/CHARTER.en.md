# Claim Commons Charter v0.1

**Working name: Claim Commons**  
**Core principle: Publish the smallest defensible claim. Preserve the full evidence trail. Let significance emerge.**

## Why this platform exists

Knowledge should not have to be declared broad, transformative, or immediately useful before it can enter the scientific record. Conventional papers often ask one finite result to carry a phenomenon, a mechanism, a universal principle, biological importance, and an application at once. When those layers are fused, the actual evidence becomes harder to inspect and reuse.

Claim Commons changes the basic unit of scientific communication from a complete narrative paper to a **Claim–Evidence Record**.

## Ten founding commitments

1. **Claims first** — each record carries one bounded, testable central claim.
2. **Evidence alignment** — every evidence item states exactly which claim it supports and under which conditions.
3. **Visible boundaries** — scope, untested cases, and explicit non-claims are first-class content.
4. **Interpretation is separate** — possible significance is welcome, but never presented as validated evidence.
5. **No significance gate** — focused, null, negative, replication, and single-result records are eligible.
6. **Novelty does not replace validity** — review asks whether the claim is supported, not whether the story is exciting.
7. **History is preserved** — corrections, narrowing, contradiction, and supersession create visible versions and relations.
8. **Atomic credit** — questions, data, analyses, proofs, replications, and reviews receive traceable credit.
9. **Human- and machine-readable** — records must be easy to understand and possible to connect into a knowledge graph.
10. **Open does not mean ungoverned** — privacy, ethics, safety, authorship, and integrity remain mandatory.

## What the platform does not require

Authors do not need to establish that a claim is the first of its kind, field-changing, broadly useful, mechanistically complete, cross-domain, or suitable for a prestige narrative.

They do need to answer:

> What exactly is the claim? Under what conditions is the evidence sufficient? What result would weaken or overturn it?

## What review evaluates

Review examines whether the claim is clear, the methods and independent units are appropriate, the evidence matches the claim strength, alternatives are tested or retained, artifacts are inspectable, and extrapolations are disclosed.

Records can be **deposited, evidence-complete, expert-checked, independently reproduced, challenged, narrowed, or superseded**. These states describe evidence history rather than a single prestige rank.

## The knowledge structure we seek

Claims may be linked through: **supports, contradicts, replicates, narrows, generalises, depends_on, and supersedes**.

The result is not a warehouse of paper titles, but a navigable argument network showing what is known, how strongly, and within which boundaries.
