# Claim Review Rubric v0.1

A reviewer does not decide whether the work is sufficiently important. The task is to evaluate the claim–evidence connection.

## Checklist

1. **Claim clarity** — Can the claim be judged without reconstructing an unstated story?
2. **Scope clarity** — Are population, model, conditions, time window, and independent units explicit?
3. **Evidence relevance** — Does each evidence item bear directly on the stated claim?
4. **Evidence sufficiency** — Does the design support the claimed strength: existence, direction, quantity, causality, mechanism, generality, or theorem?
5. **Uncertainty** — Are variability, censoring, failed runs, null results, and proof assumptions reported?
6. **Alternatives** — Are the leading alternative explanations tested or retained as open?
7. **Artifacts** — Can another researcher inspect the data, code, workflow, derivation, or proof where ethically possible?
8. **Boundary discipline** — Do the non-claims prevent common overinterpretations?
9. **Provenance** — Are contributor roles, versions, and source context visible?
10. **Integrity and ethics** — Are privacy, consent, safety, conflicts, and attribution handled appropriately?

## Review outcomes

- **Supports as scoped** — evidence supports the exact claim as written.
- **Supports after clarification** — evidence is adequate, but wording or scope needs a bounded correction.
- **Evidence gap** — a named claim requires additional evidence or a weaker formulation.
- **Contradicted** — supplied or linked evidence directly weighs against the claim.
- **Cannot assess** — essential artifacts or definitions are missing.

A review must identify the precise sentence, field, analysis, or artifact at issue. “Not important enough” is not a valid outcome.
